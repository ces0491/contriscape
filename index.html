          Guide

          The city

          Each tower is one day and each block is a year. Height is the square root of that day's contributions, measured against a busy day for that user. The rare days far above it stand only a little taller, so one huge day doesn't flatten the rest. Red lights mark the busiest days.

          Ways to fly

          Autopilot
          Replays the history as a flight. The city rises as the date passes. The ship flies above most days, so the busiest rise past it. A flight through the whole history flies down two years, the busiest and the latest busy one, and passes the others at speed. When it ends, you're offered the controls.
          Pilot
          You fly. Aim at a tower to read its date and count. A tap of a steering key nudges the nose and holding it turns harder. Gold markers show the badges still standing, with arrows at the edge of the screen for those out of view. With no badge left, a marker in the buildings' colour points back to the city once you have flown clear of it. The ship stops while a panel is open.
          Orbit
          Rotate and zoom around the finished city. Point at a tower to read it.
          Race
          Race Autopilot down a year's alleys and once round its busiest day, through gold gates in turn. The next gate is bright and has a marker, and one flown past has to be gone back for. Passing close to towers at speed scores near misses, which build a chain until two seconds pass without one, and a clip loses the chain. Your fastest run with each ship is kept, and flies alongside as a faint ghost the next time.

          Buttons

          Visit
          Opens the city of the username in the box.
          Explore
          Lists other busy cities, by field and by repo. Pick one, or let Random city choose, and it opens with a line about its owner. This city says whose city you're in, from their GitHub profile: their bio, the languages most of their repos are in, and the repos they pinned. The lists come from GitHub's search and are remade from time to time.
          Copy link
          Copies the address of this city. Larger screens only.
          Pause, Play, Replay
          Stops or resumes Autopilot. From Pilot or Orbit, Replay starts the flight again.
          All years
          Lists the years. Pick one to fly it, or All for the whole history.
          Race
          Lists the years you can race, with the rival's time and your fastest with this ship. After a race, Copy a challenge copies a link that offers the same race with your time to beat. Explore names a day's course, the same for everyone that day, and lists the cities you've flown here under Flown here.
          Timeline
          Drag it to move through the dates.
          Badges
          Lists the milestones in this history. Pick one to jump to it. The ship waits there while you line up the shot, and Shift or the Boost button flies off. Aim close to a badge and the targeting computer locks on, with a tone and the badge's name, and shots fired while locked curve into it. A badge you find by its gold marker, without the jump, is earned as flown, and the list keeps count of those. Clear a city by flying, with no jump, and it's timed: the clock is under the date, and your fastest clear is kept.
          Settings
          Sound, music, mouse steering, Invert Y, the look, the ship, and the colours of the buildings and ship. Sound starts at your first click, tap or key press, because browsers hold it back until then, and an iPhone in silent mode keeps it quiet. The music plays the history: in Autopilot each note is the busiest day just passed, higher for a busier day, and in Pilot the towers you fly over play. With mouse steering on, the ship turns towards the cursor. It's off by default, so the mouse stays free for the buttons. Look redraws the city: Night, Blocks (the city by day, in stacked blocks), Poster (flat colour on paper), Vector (glowing outlines), 16-bit (a bright afternoon in big pixels and flat bands of colour) and VGA (bolted metal walls and a painted dusk, in bigger pixels still). Ship model swaps the ship, shows it turning beside the panel, and names its power: the Interceptor locks on from further off, the Dart turns faster, the Rocket boosts faster, the Saucer brakes to a stop, and the Brick keeps its speed when it clips a tower. Each ship has its own cockpit, with a speed dial and lamps for a lock and for boost. Scroll all the way in to sit in it.
          Your year on GitHub
          Through December, and the first half of January for the year just ended, a flight through the year: its number, its count rising with its towers, its biggest moments and badges, and fireworks at the end. It flies a limited edition of its own, a ship, colours, look and music that can't be picked in Settings. The card at the end gives the year's own link, which previews as a card of the year.

          Why these badges

          These are contriscape's own badges, worked out from dates and counts in GitHub's public data, such as a first pull request, a longest streak or a busiest day. The achievements on GitHub profiles aren't shown because GitHub has no API for them, its policy only allows scraping for research and archiving, and the artwork is GitHub's.

          Shooting a badge blows it up and saves it in this browser. It changes nothing on GitHub. Reset, at the top of the Badges list, brings back the ones you've earned on this city so you can shoot them again.

          Your data

          A city shows what a public GitHub profile already shows. This site has no accounts or cookies. Vercel Web Analytics counts page views, recording the page, the site that linked here, a rough location, and the type of device and browser, without identifying you. Your badges, settings, race times and ghosts, and the cities you've flown, stay in this browser, which opens on the last of them. Fonts load from Google Fonts.
